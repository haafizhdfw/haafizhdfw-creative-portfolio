02 Strategy

Not more views.
The right views.

My goal was never to go viral. It was to build a content system where every post, every ad, and every landing page serves a specific role in the funnel — and the data tells me exactly which role is working.

The Philosophy
"1 million views gives you 10 buyers. 10,000 views give you 50 buyers. I optimize for the second scenario, every time."
The Problem

Supersub operates in construction, a high-consideration, high-ticket space ($7,900–$8,940 NZD per unit). Typical content plays don't work here. You can't entertain your way to a sale. Buyers need to trust the product, understand the specs, and believe the company delivers.

Traditional agencies treat every brand the same. Supersub needed a system built around how construction buyers actually make decisions, so I built one.

The Framework

I map every piece of content to one of 5 content pillars before production begins: Educate (25%), Problem (15%), Solution (25%), Trust (20%), Convert (15%). The question before every script: "What is the purpose of this post?"

I apply this across all channels: TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, LinkedIn, and document it inside my custom Content Dashboard for the whole team to reference.

Content Architecture for Supersub
1
Define the content universe
I mapped out 12 content categories, 5 pillars, and 120+ topic ideas for each product line (Snaplock Wall, SIP Panel). Documented in my Content Dashboard — not in someone's head.
2
Produce to purpose
Every carousel, reel, and short-form video is scripted with a clear pillar objective. Carousels handle education and trust. Reels drive reach. Ads drive intent.
3
Measure and iterate
TikTok search traffic %, Facebook CPL, GSC click growth, GA4 generate_lead events — all tracked. I cut poor-performing angles and scale the strong ones.
4
Build infrastructure to sustain it
Two custom dashboards, a CRM replacing HubSpot, and n8n automation — I built this so the system can scale without proportionally scaling headcount.
Key Differentiator
I'm not just managing posts. This is an end-to-end growth system, from content strategy to lead capture to quote delivery, that I designed, built, and operate.

Why This Number Matters
Industry benchmark for B2B construction leads: $50–$200+ per lead. At $3.87 average CPL for a $7,900–$8,940 NZD product, my cost-to-revenue ratio puts the campaign in an exceptionally efficient position. For every dollar spent, the potential return is measurably outsized.
Creative Approach

My high-performing ads used clean JPEG product photography, no motion, no heavy editing. The product speaks for itself. My job was to stop the scroll and get the right person to raise their hand.

I tested testimonial variants and lifestyle angles. Product-direct remained the strongest performer across all rounds.

Targeting & Audience

I built targeting around construction decision-makers: business owners, property developers, commercial builders in New Zealand. I refined interest and behavior layering over multiple campaign iterations.

The Shift That Matters

Traffic used to depend almost entirely on branded queries, people who already knew Supersub. That's not acquisition, it's retention.

I shifted the content strategy toward non-branded, intent-based keywords: "SIP homes NZ," "modular home comparison," "snaplock wall cost." People who have never heard of Supersub but are actively searching for what Supersub sells.

Homepage position improved from 37.23 → 15.19 in 6 months, without link building.

Data-Informed Iteration

The Modular Comparison article I wrote recorded the highest average engagement time (142 seconds) of any page. Readers finish it. But zero leads converted from it.

My diagnosis: the content is excellent, the CTA architecture is not. My next iteration targets this gap, introducing a direct path from article to quote request at the point of peak engagement.

This is what separates data-informed SEO from blog writing.
